## Lessons Learned

### 1. Match the diagram to the client's actual stack

The diagram showed Google Sheets, Gmail, and Notion as output destinations.
Tom's job post explicitly listed PHP, MySQL, and Microsoft tools.

**Rule:** Before recording, re-read the job post and make sure every tool in your
diagram mirrors what they said. Never show tools they didn't mention — it signals
you built a generic template, not a custom solution.

Fix: "Your MySQL database" — not "Google Sheets or Notion."

---

### 2. "So" is the silent proposal killer

22 filler words. 20 of them were "so." It's almost imperceptible in the moment but
compounding. One "so" is fine. Fifteen in 90 seconds reads as nervous, unprepared,
filler-thinking.

**Rule:** Practice starting every sentence without "so." If you would say "so the
system does X" — cut the "so" and start with "the system does X." Nothing is lost.
Replace "so" with a beat of silence.

---

### 3. Domain-specific labels create the "they get me" moment

The diagram said: "Action Items / Key Decisions / Anything at all."
The correct version for a law firm: "Action Items / Client Names / Next Court Dates / Billing Hours."

**Rule:** The labels inside your diagram are the most important words in the Loom.
They're what the client reads while you talk. Generic labels = generic freelancer.
Law firm → legal field names. SaaS company → SaaS field names. E-commerce → order/product fields.
15 seconds of research before recording, 10x more credibility.

---

### 4. Proposal Looms must end at 90 seconds

The Loom ran 3:19. The closing question was at 3:06. Most clients don't finish watching
an unsolicited 3-minute video from a freelancer they've never met. They check out after
90 seconds. The most important line — the question that gets a reply — was buried where
it couldn't be heard.

**Rule:** Hard stop at 90 seconds. If you can't say it in 90, you're over-explaining.
The Loom's job is to get a reply, not to close a deal. Keep something for the call.

---

### 5. Face cam must be on and lit

The camera panel was visible but completely black throughout. No face, no connection.
A voice over a screenshare is weaker than a face + screen. Trust is visual.

**Rule:** Before recording: confirm camera is on, lit from the front, not backlit.
Position the face cam in Loom's lower-left corner. Even a small clear face cam
beats a black void.

---

### 6. Irrelevant technical tangents confuse clients

The Loom included a 15-second explanation of why Claude Opus wasn't being used for this
task ("Opus is for a bigger tax, we don't need it"). Tom has no idea what Opus is and
doesn't need to. The tangent confused without informing.

**Rule:** Every sentence must answer one of three questions Tom has:
- Does this person understand what I need?
- Can they actually build it?
- What do I do next?
If a sentence doesn't answer one of those three, cut it.

---

### 7. Timing beats perfection when competition is moving

With 4+ bidders already active, sending an edited Loom was the right call over
waiting to re-record a perfect one. A proposal sent is infinitely better than
a perfect proposal sitting on your hard drive while the client interviews someone else.

**Rule:** On jobs where competition is already engaged (20+ proposals, people in
interviews), a good-enough edited Loom sent fast beats a perfect re-record sent slow.
Re-record only when there's no active competition or when the issues are severe
enough to guarantee no reply.

---

### 8. Check every label in the diagram before recording

"Get Error" was spelled "Get Errror" (triple R) in the n8n workflow node. It was
visible in the Loom. Small but tells the client you didn't proofread your own work.

**Rule:** 30-second pre-flight before recording: read every text label in the diagram
out loud. Catch typos before they're permanently on screen.

---

### 9. Open on their problem, never on yourself

"Good evening from here" was the first line. It consumed 3 seconds with zero value
to Tom. He doesn't care about Emmanuel's timezone.

**Rule:** First word of the Loom = their situation. Always.
Format: "[Their name], [their situation]. Here's how I'd fix it."
"Tom, your Zoom meetings end and someone manually pulls info from the summary.
Here's the system that does that automatically."
Done. 8 seconds. Tom is already leaning in.

## What Worked

- The Excalidraw diagram: professional, clear dual-path architecture, shows preparation
- Dual-path design (Zoom AI enabled vs ffmpeg fallback) shows real technical depth
- Error handling section at the bottom is a strong signal — most freelancers skip it
- Closing questions were the right ones ("what are you extracting / how many calls/week")
- Text wrapper was clean and tight

## Application Going Forward

Before every Loom record:
- [ ] Re-read job post — confirm diagram tools match their stack
- [ ] Check every diagram label — domain-specific, no typos
- [ ] Camera on, front-lit, small corner
- [ ] Start line written out: "[Name], [their situation]..."
- [ ] Hard stop planned at 90 seconds
- [ ] Zero "so" allowed — practice the first 3 sentences without it
