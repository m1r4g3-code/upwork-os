## The Six-Figure Deal Pattern (How Ryan Closes $100k+ Clients)

**The single most important insight from all his content:** Every six-figure deal he has ever closed came through an **inbound invitation**, not a proposal he sent. The $150k, $160k, $180k, and $200k+ deals all started with the client reaching out to him. Profile boosting and keyword ranking is what triggers this. Proposals are the path to $10-15k. Inbound is the path to $100k+.

### The $15k Live Close — Step by Step

1. Client sent invitation (Ryan's profile ranked for their search)
2. 60+ people had already applied. They reached out to Ryan personally.
3. Ryan sent a short, casual Loom — not a text proposal. Just: "Hey, I can definitely help, see my portfolio, let's chat, not much context here so message me."
4. Client replied and asked to book a call
5. Ryan sent **one scheduling link** — Upwork scheduler or Zoom. No back and forth.
6. On the call: brief rapport (where are you from, I'm in Thailand, etc.) → "Tell me more about the project and how I can help" → **shut up and let them talk as long as possible**
7. More they talk = more they invest = more likely to hire
8. Ryan listened for: what's the scope, what are the pain points, what could we charge
9. After call: used **Fathom** (call recorder) to get transcript → pasted into **Manis** (AI tool) → Manis generated the SOW and wireframes
10. Sent wireframes to client before formal quote
11. Client insisted on NDA before sharing details. Ryan: "Send me a $10 contract so I can share my email" → signed, emailed, NDA signed
12. Booked another call → closed
13. Payment: ~40% upfront ($4,500), rest at milestones
14. Others bid $4-5k. He bid $15k. He got it.

### The $180k Deal — How the Big Ones Work

What looked like a $30k project became $180k for 6 months. It took 10 hours of calls to close. Ryan wasn't on all the calls — he had others take some. Before a single contract was signed, he built a **free proof of concept worth $5-10k**. Multiple calls, weeks of work, zero income — before the deal. That's how he gets $180k when others can't get $15k.

**The principle:** Quote under, deliver over. Tell them 12 weeks, deliver in 8. Zero churn. That's how you get repeat business and referrals to fill the next slot.

## Profile System — The 10-Stage Checklist

### Stage 1: Research

**Step 1:** Upwork search → Talent → your niche → filter Earned Amount → $10K → edit URL, add a zero → see $100K+ earners. Bookmark them all. Study: title, first line, description structure, portfolio, skills. Copy what works.

**Step 2:** Create a **client account** and post a fake job in your niche. Realistic, looks real. Invite all the top profiles you bookmarked. They will apply. Read their proposals. See what's converting. This is how Ryan maps the entire competitive landscape in 24 hours.

### Stage 2: The Big Four

1. **100% profile completion** — required before Rising Talent, Top Rated, Top Rated Plus
2. **Keywords everywhere** — Ryan has "AI" 24 times, "automation" 18 times. Put it in: title, description (including bottom spam), portfolio titles, portfolio descriptions, skills in each portfolio piece, certifications (2 free ones), education, work history, job contract titles
3. **Shorten name** — Jatinder → J got more messages immediately. If your name is long or hard to pronounce, abbreviate it
4. **Rising Talent badge** — appears after 100% completion + activity. Clients don't know what it means but a badge = credibility signal

### Stage 3: Profile Picture

- Shoulder up, camera facing you, natural smile
- Add a ring/border around it in Canva — stands out in search results
- Do NOT use AI background removal with colored replacement — looks low quality
- Candid-style > formal headshot
- People buy from who they like. The profile picture is a trust signal.

### Stage 4: Video Introduction

- A Loom video scrolling through your profile and showing your work
- Do AFTER the rest of the profile is filled out — more to show, looks more credible
- Main goal: show you are personable, reliable, and can do the job
- Keep it short — this is not a sales pitch, it is a trust builder

### Stage 5: Profile Description

- Do NOT use ChatGPT
- First line: state exactly what you do and the outcome you deliver
  - Ryan: "Give me something to build and I'll build it so it works for you 24/7. That's my promise."
- Spam keywords at the bottom (a list of tools and skills — looks bad but works algorithmically)
- Full alignment: description keywords must match portfolio keywords must match jobs completed

## Loom Video System

### Two types of jobs:

**Context jobs** (link, business name, person's name, anything Google-able in the post):
- Full audit Loom (60-90 seconds)
- Open on their website on screen
- Break down: website speed, their ads, their competitors
- Show one portfolio piece
- Close with a low-friction question
- Customize Loom title: client's first name + their niche
- Use rebrandly to make custom URL (yourname.com/upworkproposal — looks professional)

**No-context jobs** (80% of jobs):
- Short Loom under 45 seconds
- Screen recording over their job post, you in corner
- "Hey, there isn't enough context here for me to give you a full audit. If you can send your website or social links, I'll do that for you. Where are you from by the way? I can see you're in [country]."
- Goal: get them to reply and send context → then you do the full audit

**The Loom text wrapper (the ENTIRE written proposal):**
```
Hey [name],

I made you a personalized Loom. Check it out: [link]

P.S. [one personalized line — where they're from, something from their post]
```

Do NOT put ChatGPT text around the Loom. It buries the video and kills the proposal.

### Loom tracking:
- Loom sends email notifications when someone watches your video
- Track views in Loom dashboard
- Target: 10 Looms per day

## Mindset — Developer to Deal Maker

From the $70k developer video: the biggest shift is not technical, it is mental.

- Stop thinking like a developer executing code
- Start thinking like a PM and deal maker: scope, expectations, client management
- Setting good expectations > writing beautiful code
- Clients often don't scope perfectly — go above and beyond instead of exploiting the gap
- Over-deliver on every contract, push the team hard to make it happen
- Over-delivery → 5-star reviews → repeat business → referrals
- Ryan was NOT technical. Can read HTML/CSS. Used a 2-3 person team. Made $70K/month.
